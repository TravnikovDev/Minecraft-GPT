# Format and platform strategy for vertical devlog Shorts about an autonomous AI Minecraft bot (state: 26 November 2026)

Method notes for the report writer:
- "Channel data" below means public YouTube listings read on 2026-11-26 (channel Shorts/Videos tabs and individual video pages; YouTube rounds counts in listings). These are primary observations, but they are a small hand-picked sample, not a study.
- "Official" = a platform's own help/policy/blog page that was opened. "Press" = news coverage of an official statement. "Vendor" = a tool-seller or marketing blog; treat every vendor number as low confidence.
- Where only a search snippet was seen (page not opened or not readable), it is marked "(snippet only)".

## 1. Comparable creators: who makes AI-plays-Minecraft / bot / AI-agent content, and what do their short vertical videos look like?

### Takeaway
The proven hits in this niche are long-form "experiment" videos about what the AI does (1M-11M views); nobody found in this research has built a successful short-vertical devlog series about an LLM Minecraft bot, and the closest project (Emergent Garden / Mindcraft) gets only 3K-20K views on its Minecraft Shorts despite 294K subscribers. The short-form models that demonstrably work nearby are (a) a fixed-title weekly voiced devlog series (Inbound Shovel, 76-100 s, 160K-1.4M views each), (b) "AI Learns to X" visual payoff clips (AI Warehouse, 40-49 s, up to 11.8M), and (c) 13-20 s personality/fail clips (Neuro-sama, 100K-650K).

### Inferences
- The gap in the market is real: searches for short vertical devlogs of an LLM Minecraft bot returned no established series. The format has to be borrowed from adjacent winners rather than copied from a direct competitor.
- Emergent Garden's Shorts suggest that raw, untitled, square clips of bot behaviour do not travel even with a large subscriber base; the one Short with a narrative title ("Justice for Gemini") had a like rate about four times higher. Whether those clips had narration was not checked.
- Inbound Shovel's pattern (one named series, one topic per episode, own voice over gameplay, weekly, 75-100 s) is the best-evidenced template for a devlog Short. Its use of the creator's own voice and the on-screen style are from general knowledge of the channel, not verified in this session.
- A big long-form audience does not transfer to Shorts (Code Bullet: 3.47M subs, 5K-45K Short views), so Shorts should be designed as their own product, not cut-downs.
- The Warboy devlogs fail on every packaging dimension at once (horizontal, 720p, project-name-first titles, 2-5 min, no hook in the title), which is consistent with packaging, not concept, being the limiter. This is an inference from titles and metadata only.
- Do not use #mindcraft as a discovery tag.

### Gaps
- Voice type (own voice vs TTS), face/no-face share and on-screen-text style for each channel were not verified by watching the videos; only metadata was read.
- No TikTok or Instagram data for these creators was collected (those pages were not accessible to the tools used). Posting cadence there is unknown.
- No Voyager, Altera/Project Sid or other lab accounts with a vertical series were found; no view data for them.
- Social Blade history (growth over time) was not accessible.

## 2. Structure: hooks, pacing, on-screen text, captions, loops, endings and CTAs

### Takeaway
There is no official or rigorous public data on hook timing or ideal length; the only solid facts are YouTube's own metrics ("viewed vs swiped away", engaged views) and its advice to verbally point to the Related Video link. The one sizeable CTA dataset (vendor, Nov 2026) says an end-of-video ask is roughly neutral-to-slightly-negative for views (0.96x) while a mid-video "follow" ask after a payoff correlates with more views (1.37x), so the evidence favours a short ask before the visual ending rather than a tacked-on "please subscribe" outro.

### Cited Findings

Official
- YouTube Analytics reports, for each Short, the share of viewers who "chose to view" versus swiped away — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281) (snippet only)
- YouTube publishes no universal good/bad cutoff for viewed-vs-swiped-away — [Creator Essentials glossary](https://www.creatoressentials.com/glossary/viewed-vs-swiped-away/) (vendor, snippet only)
- Since 31 March 2025 a Shorts "view" counts every play or replay with no minimum watch time; the old metric is now "engaged views" and is what YPP eligibility and revenue use — [TechCrunch, 26 Mar 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/26/youtube-is-changing-how-youtube-shorts-views-are-counted)
- YouTube's Julia Lee Harter (Culture and Entertainment Editor), July 2026: use the Related Video link to bridge Shorts to long-form, and call it out in the Short (point at it or show the thumbnail); "The very first few seconds of your long video should confirm they made the right choice." — [Social Media Today, 19 Jul 2026](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-offers-tips-on-working-with-shorts-videos/825096/)
- Same source: reply to comments and make standout commenters "the star of your next video"; poll, quiz and Q&A stickers are suggested for interaction — [Social Media Today, 19 Jul 2026](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-offers-tips-on-working-with-shorts-videos/825096/)

CTA evidence (vendor studies)
- The Content Labs (published 17 Nov 2026): 4,914 short videos from 76 creators across TikTok, Instagram, YouTube, Facebook and X, each compared with its creator's median views. Follow ask 1.25x; no ask 1.05x; comment ask 1.03x; click/buy link ask 0.82x. Placement: ask in the middle 1.37x (905 videos); ask at the end 0.96x (1,882 videos). "Subscribe" was not a separate category — [The Content Labs](https://thecontentlabs.app/blog/call-to-action-data-study)
- Caveats stated by that study: sample skews to commentary, sports and news; correlation, not causation; the metric is views, not retention; the mid-video result depends on having an early payoff to place the ask after — [The Content Labs](https://thecontentlabs.app/blog/call-to-action-data-study)
- A secondary write-up of a "RetentionRail" study of 50,000 videos (Jan-Mar 2026) says TikTok's two danger zones are the first two seconds and the final 10%, "where post-call-to-action drift kills the finish" — [Kinovela](https://kinovela.com/insights/short-form-video-retention-benchmarks) (vendor, secondary, snippet only; original not located)
- Claim that a bare "subscribe" with no reason has almost no effect on subscription rate cites an unnamed study — [Zebracat](https://www.zebracat.ai/post/best-youtube-shorts-call-to-actions) (vendor, unsourced, snippet only)

Hook evidence (vendor folklore)
- "Shorts with an immediate hook in the first 2 seconds retain 19% more viewers than those with a slow start" (Zebracat 2025, as cited by Virvid) — [Virvid](https://virvid.ai/blog/first-3-seconds-hook-faceless-shorts-2026) (vendor, snippet only)
- "If more than 50% of viewers leave in the first 3 seconds, the first distribution test is likely dead" comes from a TikTok-focused vendor report, and a "<30% swipe-away" target appears with no source — [dev.to post](https://dev.to/bulkdl/hook-engineering-the-science-of-the-first-3-seconds-in-short-form-video-2026-2jp9) (vendor, snippet only)

Length evidence (contradictory)
- OverseerOS 2026 analysis of 16,537 Shorts: the 16-30 s bucket had the highest median views; the study states it could not see retention, swipes or completion — [OverseerOS](https://www.overseeros.com/blog/best-youtube-shorts-length) (vendor, snippet only)
- An Inflow Network analysis of 5,400 Shorts (as cited by Piktochart) found 50-60 s videos earned about 22x the views of clips under 10 s — [Piktochart](https://piktochart.com/blog/how-long-youtube-shorts/) (vendor, secondary, snippet only)
- Adobe Express survey (March 2026): 46% of creators believe under 30 s is the ideal Shorts length (opinion, not measurement) — [Adobe Express](https://www.adobe.com/express/learn/blog/youtube-shorts-length-study) (snippet only)
- Buffer, 2.7M Instagram Reels (1 Jan-15 Aug 2026), reach versus each account's own baseline: 90-179 s +7.4%; 30-59 s +2.7%; 60-89 s +1.6%; 15-29 s -0.9%; under 15 s -1.5%; 180 s or more -49.3% — [Buffer, 12 Nov 2026](https://buffer.com/resources/instagram-reels-length/)

Observed in comparable channels (channel data, 2026-11-26)
- Working devlog Shorts run 76-100 s (Inbound Shovel); working "AI learns" Shorts run 40-49 s (AI Warehouse); working clip Shorts run 13-20 s (Neuro-sama) — [Inbound Shovel](https://www.youtube.com/@InboundShovel/shorts), [AI Warehouse](https://www.youtube.com/@aiwarehouse/shorts), [Neuro-sama](https://www.youtube.com/@Neurosama/shorts)
- Title framings that reached 1M+ in this niche: "<N> AIs Survive <N> Days in Minecraft", "AI Plays Minecraft Forever (and dies)", "I let AI play Minecraft <mode>", "An AI Joined My Minecraft World...", "AI Learns to <verb>", "AI Tries to Speedrun Minecraft" — [Emergent Garden](https://www.youtube.com/@EmergentGarden/videos), [GoldActual](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RQqqm7qk8bk), [Kolanii](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=g2T-2-mvQgk), [AI Warehouse](https://www.youtube.com/@aiwarehouse/shorts), [Wifies](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1Sf437NKUPs)
- Fail/odd-moment titles outperform neutral ones inside the same series: Emergent Garden "church fail" 17K vs "church decent" 10K vs "useless talk" 2.7K — [Shorts tab](https://www.youtube.com/@EmergentGarden/shorts)

### Inferences
- Length should follow the format, not a universal number: a narrated "what changed" devlog fits 60-100 s (Inbound Shovel; Buffer's Reels data also favours 90-179 s), a single funny bot moment fits 13-30 s. Keeping every cut under 3 minutes (and ideally at or above 60 s if TikTok rewards matter later) satisfies all three platforms.
- For the planned "ending in a follow/subscribe CTA": the available evidence says put the reason-to-follow ask right after the main payoff or inside the last line while footage is still running, and do not add a separate outro card. A series promise ("next: I give it a sword") doubles as the reason. On YouTube, the CTA with official backing is pointing at the Related Video link.
- "Day N of teaching an AI to play Minecraft", "I gave my bot X and it did Y" and before/after are consistent with the title patterns that worked long-form, but no data was found that tests these framings as Shorts series in this niche.
- First-seconds advice (open on the result or the failure, state the premise in on-screen text) is plausible and universal among vendors but unquantified; the creator's own "viewed vs swiped away" figure is the only trustworthy measure.

### Gaps
- No official YouTube, TikTok or Meta statement was found on hook timing, loops, on-screen text density or caption style. Loop benefits ("100%+ retention under 30 s") are vendor rules of thumb.
- No study isolating an explicit "subscribe" ask on Shorts retention was found.
- Claim that auto-captions are now on by default on all three apps (vidpros) could not be confirmed against a platform source.

## 3. Is the audience more interested in the bot's behaviour or in the code/architecture?

### Takeaway
Behaviour, by a wide margin, for reach: on the closest comparable channel the behaviour experiments get 100K-1.9M views while the project update and research-paper videos get 29K-74K, and the multi-million-view videos elsewhere are all "what did the AI do" stories. Engineering content can still work as Shorts when each episode is one concrete, plainly explained problem (Inbound Shovel).

### Cited Findings
- Emergent Garden, same channel, same audience: "4 AIs Survive 10 Days in Minecraft" 1.9M and "AI Plays Minecraft Forever (and dies)" 659K, versus "Vision and Vibe Coding | Mindcraft Update" 74K and "Mindcraft Research Paper!" 29K (read 2026-11-26) — [channel Videos tab](https://www.youtube.com/@EmergentGarden/videos)
- The practical how-to sits in between: "How to Play Minecraft with AI (mindcraft tutorial)" 228K; a third-party tutorial "How to play Minecraft with ChatGPT (mindcraft bot tutorial)" by riqvip has 22.8K — [Emergent Garden](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gRotoL8P8D8), [riqvip](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=engH5lQky6U)
- The top "AI in Minecraft" videos are framed as social/behaviour stories: Kolanii 11.2M ("An AI Joined My Minecraft World... It Was Terrifying"), Species 5.98M ("...They Created A Civilization"), Wifies 3.93M ("AI Simulates Civilization in Minecraft") — [Kolanii](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=g2T-2-mvQgk), [Species](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uRDBco-cSK4), [Wifies](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OSGSROIs8cI)
- Neuro-sama's Shorts are all personality moments, 100K-650K each, with no technical content in titles — [Shorts tab](https://www.youtube.com/@Neurosama/shorts)
- Counter-evidence that engineering topics can carry Shorts: Inbound Shovel's "Game Dev Secrets" episodes on save files (687K), keybindings (501K), sub-pixel animation (578K), Steam patching (1.0M); the most code-flavoured title, "Async/Await/Ahelpme", is the lowest of the sample at 156K — [Shorts tab](https://www.youtube.com/@InboundShovel/shorts)
- Technique-in-the-title also works when the payoff is visible behaviour: "AI Learns Minecraft PvP (Reinforcement Learning)" 176K; "My Computer Taught Itself to Play Minecraft" 1.05M — [Kadambi Engineering](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j5nxDKAjg6U), [Poppro](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IQ7sK6PezJo)
- Feature-list devlog titles with the product name first get almost nothing: "PingMe DevLog | UPDATE PAID VERSION | ADD BOT VIEW | ADD MANAGING BOTS FROM CHAT" 459 views — [video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-UX2yOSZ57s)
- Indie-dev guides name bug showcases and before/after clips as top short-form performers — [presskit.gg](https://presskit.gg/field-guides/tiktok-indie-game-marketing) (vendor, snippet only)

### Inferences
- Lead every Short with what the bot did (or failed to do); use architecture as the one-sentence "why" in the middle. "What changed" should be shown as changed behaviour (before/after), not as a changelog.
- Code-on-screen is best kept to a few seconds as proof; the people who want architecture are the ones to send to GitHub/long-form.
- Two audiences exist: a broad Minecraft/AI-curious audience (behaviour) and a much smaller builder audience (how-to, repo). The 228K tutorial shows the second is not negligible for a GitHub project.

### Gaps
- No comment-level or survey evidence of viewer motivation was collected; the conclusion rests on view counts of a small sample.
- No retention data comparing gameplay segments with code segments was found.

## 4. Platform specs and limits in late 2026

### Takeaway
A single 1080x1920, 9:16, H.264 MP4 of up to 3 minutes is accepted as a Short, a TikTok and a recommendable Reel; the hard platform facts are YouTube's 3-minute Shorts limit and Instagram's "over 3 minutes won't be recommended" rule. Reposting your own video across platforms is not prohibited by any rule found, but visible third-party watermarks are disqualifying on Instagram and for TikTok rewards, and safe-zone pixel numbers are vendor estimates that disagree.

### Cited Findings

YouTube Shorts (official)
- Shorts can be up to three minutes; square or vertical videos up to three minutes uploaded on or after 15 October 2024 are classified as Shorts; wider ratios (e.g. 16:9) stay long-form — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877?hl=en)
- A Short longer than one minute with any active Content ID or manual copyright claim is blocked globally (not playable, recommended or monetized), with no channel penalty; Shorts of one minute or less are not affected — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877?hl=en)
- Shorts Audio Library songs can usually be used for up to 90 s in a three-minute Short (some 60 or 30 s); YouTube Audio Library royalty-free music gets no Content ID claim — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877?hl=en)

Instagram Reels
- Reels can be up to 20 minutes (rolled out gradually; some accounts still see 90 s or 3 min caps); the app warns "Reels over 3 minutes won't be recommended to new audiences" — [Buffer, 12 Nov 2026](https://buffer.com/resources/instagram-reels-length/) (vendor quoting an in-app alert)
- Reels of 180 s or longer reached 49.3% fewer accounts than the account's baseline in Buffer's 2.7M-Reel dataset (Jan-Aug 2026) — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/instagram-reels-length/)
- Instagram's eligibility wording for recommended content, as quoted from its help page: original, within Community Guidelines, "has no visible watermarks" and meets the Recommendation Guidelines — [Junibloom quoting Instagram help](https://www.junibloom.com/blog/instagram-trial-reels-0-views) (secondary, snippet only)
- 30 April 2026 (official): originality rules extended from Reels to photos and carousels; accounts that mostly post non-original content are removed from recommendations; "original" means created by you or reflecting your own perspective; adding only a border, watermark, caption or credit does not make someone else's content original; status is recalculated over a 30-day period and can be appealed in Account Status — [Instagram Creators blog](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/rewarding-original-creators-on-instagram)
- That page does not address cross-posting your own content from another platform — [Instagram Creators blog](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/rewarding-original-creators-on-instagram)
- Trial Reels (shown to non-followers first) exist; sources conflict on duration (48 vs 72 h) and eligibility (200 vs 1,000 followers); a trial "may get limited reach" if Instagram detects the same content was shared before — [Junibloom](https://www.junibloom.com/blog/instagram-trial-reels-0-views), [Fliki](https://fliki.ai/blog/trial-reels-instagram) (vendor, snippet only)

TikTok
- TikTok allows about 10 minutes recorded in-app and 60 minutes uploaded — [Buffer comparison table](https://buffer.com/resources/instagram-reels-length/) (vendor, single secondary source, snippet only)
- File-size limits are reported inconsistently (287.6 MB mobile / 2 GB desktop in one guide); TikTok re-encodes every upload — [Sprout Social specs guide, updated Oct-Nov 2026](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/social-media-video-specs/) (vendor, snippet only)

Common export and safe zones (vendor)
- Guides agree on 1080x1920, 9:16, MP4/H.264, 30 or 60 fps as a file that uploads cleanly to Shorts, TikTok, Instagram Reels and Facebook Reels — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-shorts-vertical-video) (vendor, snippet only)
- One 2026 guide gives a "universal" safe area of 900x1400 px centred in 1080x1920, with per-app areas of 900x1492 (TikTok), 996x1400 (Reels), 984x1500 (Shorts) and bottom UI bands of about 320 px (TikTok), 310 px (Reels), 300 px (Shorts) — [AdaptlyPost](https://adaptlypost.com/en/blog/social-media-safe-zones-2026-complete-guide) (vendor, snippet only)
- Another 2026 guide refuses to give one permanent pixel box and advises previewing each upload because overlays vary by app version and device — [reap.video](https://reap.video/blog/short-form-video-safe-zones) (vendor, snippet only)
- AI Warehouse uploads Shorts at 2160x3840 and they are served as such, so YouTube accepts 4K vertical — [video metadata](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/LXosuQEOLn0)

Originality / cross-posting
- 1 October 2026: YouTube is "updating our recommendation systems to further prioritize original content" in Shorts and to "reduce the reach of content re-uploaded from other creators without adding anything of your own"; the coverage says nothing about a creator's own video cross-posted from TikTok, watermarks, or monetization — [Social Media Today, 1 Oct 2026](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-updates-shorts-algorithm-to-put-more-focus-on-original-content/831976/)
- TikTok Creator Rewards-eligible videos cannot carry watermarks — [Toptal guide](https://www.toptal.com/creator/post/how-to-join-the-tiktok-creator-rewards-program) (vendor, snippet only)

### Inferences
- Export one clean master without any platform watermark or burned-in platform UI and upload it natively to each app; all located originality rules target other people's content and visible watermarks, not a creator's own file on several platforms.
- Keep captions and key action inside roughly the central 900x1400 px and keep the bottom ~320 px and the right edge free; treat that as a conservative working box and verify by previewing in each app.
- A 1-minute-plus Short must contain no claimable audio (game music is fine under Mojang's rules but any third-party track is a risk); use YouTube Audio Library or no music.
- Minecraft footage recorded at 60 fps is acceptable everywhere; no official bitrate recommendation specific to Shorts was located, so the standard YouTube upload bitrates for 1080p apply by default (not verified here).

### Gaps
- No official TikTok page on maximum length, file size or watermark demotion could be opened (TikTok support pages returned empty content to the fetch tool).
- No official safe-zone template from YouTube, TikTok or Meta for organic (non-ad) posts was found.
- No evidence, official or measured, that posting the identical file to several platforms is penalised; equally no official statement that it is safe.
- Official recommended bitrate for Shorts not found.

## 5. Policies that bite: AI disclosure, "inauthentic content", AI labels, made-for-kids, Mojang and OpenAI naming

### Takeaway
Gameplay footage of an AI bot needs no "altered content" disclosure on YouTube, and using AI tools or a synthetic voice is not itself a monetization problem; the risk is a channel that looks template-made and interchangeable from video to video. The two issues that need a decision before launch are the name (Mojang forbids "Minecraft" as the primary/dominant part of a name and OpenAI does not permit "GPT" in product names, so "MinecraftGPT" conflicts with both) and audience setting (set "not made for kids" deliberately; a made-for-kids flag removes comments, the bell, end screens and playlist saves).

### Cited Findings

YouTube: altered or synthetic content disclosure (official, read Nov 2026)
- "We require creators to disclose AI-generated or altered content that seems realistic": a real person appearing to say/do something, altered footage of real events/places, a realistic scene that did not occur — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491?hl=en)
- No disclosure is needed for clearly unrealistic or animated content, for AI help with scripts, outlines, titles, thumbnails or ideas, for cloning your own voice for voice-overs or dubs, or for gameplay footage from video games — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491?hl=en)
- "Disclosing AI content alone won't limit a video's audience or impact its eligibility to earn money." Consistent non-disclosure where it is required can lead to a label being applied, content removal or YPP suspension — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491?hl=en)

YouTube: inauthentic content and reused content (official text plus press)
- On 15 July 2025 the "repetitious content" rule was renamed "inauthentic content"; YouTube called it a clarification, not a new policy; it covers "repetitive or mass-produced content" — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-inauthentic-repeated-content/752892/) (press, snippet only), [YouTube channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- Policy text: not monetizable is content that "looks like it's made with a template", "Similar or repetitive content with low educational value, commentary, narratives, or minimal variation across videos", "Image slideshows, templated storylines, or scrolling text with minimal or no narrative, commentary, or educational value", and "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates" — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- Explicitly allowed: the same intro and outro across videos when the bulk differs, and series where "each video has a distinct storyline, focus, or concept"; creators using "automated tools or templates" must still show their own creative vision — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- The policy text does not mention synthetic voice or text-to-speech at all — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- Reused content policy "applies to your channel as a whole"; not allowed includes content "that exclusively features readings of other materials you did not originally create" and copied content "without any substantive modifications" — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- 16 July 2026 clarification: three categories cannot monetize: repetitive/generic/template-based content that is easy to mass-produce with AI, CGI or templates; "off-putting" emotionally manipulative content; AI personas of real people discussing finance, legal or health topics. YouTube's Matt Halprin said AI can enhance creativity and the target is "content farming" — [TechCrunch, 20 Jul 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/20/youtube-clarifies-policies-around-ai-slop-and-upsetting-videos/)
- Mojang's own rule points the same way: to monetize Minecraft footage you must "add enough of your own unique content", e.g. your own audio commentary; "You can't just include your logo, web address, or overlays on top of our or someone else's gameplay" — [Minecraft Usage Guidelines](https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/usage-guidelines)

TikTok and Meta AI labelling
- TikTok requires creators to label AI content that includes realistic images, audio or video (label meaning "completely generated or significantly edited by AI"); TikTok may apply an automatic label that the creator cannot remove — [TikTok Support](https://support.tiktok.com/en/using-tiktok/creating-videos/ai-generated-content) (official page, snippet only; the page itself could not be opened)
- Meta may require an AI label for photorealistic video or realistic-sounding audio that was digitally created or altered; its examples include a realistic AI-generated voiceover in a reel; cartoon-style video does not need one; penalties can apply for missing required labels; Meta also auto-labels ("AI info") — [Meta Help Centre](https://www.meta.com/help/artificial-intelligence/1783222608822690/) (official page, snippet only; the page itself could not be opened)

Made for kids / COPPA (official)
- Factors include child-directed subject matter, intended or actual child audience, "characters, celebrities, or toys that appeal to children, including animated characters", simple games, and language aimed at children — [YouTube Help: determining if content is made for kids](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9528076?hl=en)
- YouTube's own examples of general audience (set as not made for kids): animated content that appeals to everyone, and a gaming video with adult humor — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9528076?hl=en)
- On made-for-kids videos these are off: comments, notification bell, cards and end screens, saving to playlists/Watch Later, personalized ads, miniplayer, live chat, memberships, Super Chat, merchandise, home-page autoplay; a made-for-kids channel also loses Posts — [YouTube Help: set your audience](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9527654?hl=en)
- YouTube uses machine learning to find videos "clearly aimed at young audiences" and may override the creator's setting on "error or abuse"; an overridden video shows "Set to Made for Kids" and can be appealed once per video — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9527654?hl=en)
- The FTC says elements "particularly appealing to children" (characters, games, toys, songs) are weighed, and suggests checking how others view your content and similar content — [FTC business blog, Nov 2019](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2019/11/youtube-channel-owners-your-content-directed-children)
- Mojang requires that sponsors and callouts in Minecraft videos "must be suitable for children and minors" — [Minecraft Usage Guidelines](https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/usage-guidelines)

Mojang naming and branding (official page text read 2026-11-26; footer "© 2026 Mojang AB")
- "You may use the Minecraft name in a secondary name, secondary title, or description" if it is needed to describe the creation honestly, the Minecraft part "is not the dominant element or the distinctive part of the complete name or title", and no other Mojang brand element or asset is used in the branding "including as a logo or part of a logo" — [Minecraft Usage Guidelines](https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/usage-guidelines)
- "You may not use the Minecraft name as the primary or dominant name or title." Examples: "Kotoba Miners: A Minecraft server for Redstone builds" (allowed); "Minecraft - the ultimate help app" (not allowed); "The Shaft - a Minecrafter's podcast" (allowed) — [Minecraft Usage Guidelines](https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/usage-guidelines)
- "Our name" includes "any names which are confusingly similar to our name" — [Minecraft Usage Guidelines](https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/usage-guidelines)
- Required disclaimer for products/services: "NOT AN OFFICIAL MINECRAFT [PRODUCT/SERVICE/EVENT/etc.]. NOT APPROVED BY OR ASSOCIATED WITH MOJANG OR MICROSOFT" on the product, listing, description, website and related materials — [Minecraft Usage Guidelines](https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/usage-guidelines)
- Videos: you may post footage and earn ad revenue if videos are free to view, you add your own unique content, title cards/credits/sponsorship notices sit outside the actual game content, and the videos do not exist to advertise products or services unrelated to Minecraft — [Minecraft Usage Guidelines](https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/usage-guidelines)
- "Commercial use" is defined as any shared use of the name, brand or assets "regardless of whether you receive payment or provide it for free" — [Minecraft Usage Guidelines](https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/usage-guidelines)

OpenAI brand guidelines (official page, read Nov 2026)
- "we do not permit our GPT brand to be used in app, product, developer or company names"; "We do not permit model names in app titles because there is concern that it confuses end users"; "Do not feature our Marks more prominently than your own company's name or marks" — [OpenAI brand guidelines](https://openai.com/brand/)
- Referencing a model is allowed if "precise and accurate" — [OpenAI brand guidelines](https://openai.com/brand/)
- The guidelines' do/don't examples as recorded in April 2023: do "Meowlytics powered by GPT-4", "Built with", "Developed on"; don't "MeowlyticsGPT", "Meowlytics with GPT-4", "ChatGPT-powered Meowlytics" — [Classmethod summary, 26 Apr 2023](https://dev.classmethod.jp/articles/about-openai-brand-guidelines/) (secondary; the current official page hides these behind "Read more")

### Inferences
- A devlog series where each episode shows a different behaviour and has original narration fits YouTube's "allowed" examples. The danger pattern is an automated pipeline producing near-identical clips with a generic TTS script; a consistent intro/outro and series title are explicitly fine.
- TTS narration: YouTube neither requires disclosure for it in gameplay videos nor names it in monetization policy. On Instagram/Facebook a realistic AI voiceover is an example Meta says needs the AI label, and TikTok's rule covers realistic AI audio, so a TTS-voiced cut should carry the AI label on those two platforms. The creator's own voice avoids the question everywhere.
- "MinecraftGPT" / "Minecraft-GPT" as a channel or product name runs against both rulebooks: "Minecraft" is the dominant, distinctive first element (the "Minecraft - the ultimate help app" pattern), and "GPT" in a product name is the "MeowlyticsGPT" pattern. A compliant shape is "<own distinctive name>: an AI bot for Minecraft, built with <model>", with the not-official disclaimer in the channel description and repo. This is a reading of published guidelines, not legal advice; no enforcement cases against YouTube channel names were researched.
- Do not use the Minecraft logo, font or textures in the channel avatar/banner as a logo element.
- Made-for-kids: set the channel-level audience to "not made for kids" and avoid child-directed packaging (kid-voiced TTS, toy-like thumbnails, nursery-style music, #kids tags). The programming/AI framing and spoken technical commentary support a general-audience classification. Losing comments and the bell would remove exactly the feedback and return-viewer mechanics a devlog depends on.

### Gaps
- No source specific to Minecraft videos being auto-classified as made for kids was found; the mechanism above is the general one.
- TikTok's and Meta's official AI-label pages could not be opened directly; details rest on search snippets of those pages. A single blog mentions a TikTok rule version effective 24 September 2026; not verified.
- The original YouTube blog/help text of the July 2026 clarification was not opened; TechCrunch is the source.
- Whether OpenAI or Mojang have acted against small YouTube channels over names is unknown.
- TikTok/Meta treatment of obviously synthetic (non-realistic) TTS voices is not spelled out in the material seen.

## 6. Growth mechanics for a brand-new channel in 2026

### Takeaway
Platforms publish almost nothing quantitative: YouTube does not disclose the size of a Short's first test audience or the thresholds to widen it, and no official posting-frequency number was found. Vendor benchmarks put a new channel's Shorts at 50-500 views in 48 hours with 20-50 uploads of noise before a pattern shows; the only official conversion tool from Shorts to long-form is the Related Video link, called out verbally.

### Cited Findings
- YouTube does not publish the size of the first audience a Short is shown to, the response needed to expand, or the number of rounds; "seed audience" descriptions are vendor interpretations — [vidIQ, Shorts algorithm 2026](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-shorts-algorithm/) (vendor, snippet only)
- Vendor benchmark: for channels under 1,000 subscribers, 50-500 views per Short in the first 48 hours is the normal range; a "healthy" average percentage viewed is 70% or more — [Humble&Brag benchmarks 2026](https://humbleandbrag.com/blog/youtube-shorts-benchmarks) (vendor, snippet only)
- Vendor claim: the first 20-50 Shorts of a new channel perform inconsistently while the system learns who the content is for — [Humble&Brag new-channel benchmarks](https://humbleandbrag.com/blog/new-youtube-channel-average-views) (vendor, snippet only)
- Vendor claim: subscribers gained from Shorts convert to long-form views at a fraction of the rate of long-form-acquired subscribers — [Miraflow](https://miraflow.ai/blog/why-your-youtube-shorts-stop-at-1k-2k-views-in-2026) (vendor opinion, snippet only)
- Frequency advice from vendors ranges from 2-4 a week to about one a day for new channels; none is an official statement — [flowshorts](https://flowshorts.app/blog/how-many-shorts-per-day), [gtstu.com](https://gtstu.com/indie-game-devlog-audience-growth-youtube-tiktok/) (vendor, snippet only)
- Observed cadences of working channels: Inbound Shovel one Short per week; AI Warehouse 7 Shorts in total; Neuro-sama several clips per month (read 2026-11-26) — [Inbound Shovel](https://www.youtube.com/@InboundShovel/shorts), [AI Warehouse](https://www.youtube.com/@aiwarehouse/shorts), [Neuro-sama](https://www.youtube.com/@Neurosama/shorts)
- A 2,080-subscriber channel reached 215K views with one "I Let ChatGPT Play Minecraft" video, showing a single video can far exceed channel size in this niche — [video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AFXzhNyhyzk)
- Official conversion path: the Related Video link from a Short to one of your own videos, with a spoken/pointed call-out; the long video's first seconds should confirm the Short's promise — [Social Media Today quoting YouTube's Julia Lee Harter, 19 Jul 2026](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-offers-tips-on-working-with-shorts-videos/825096/)
- The Related Video link is set in YouTube Studio after upload and only points to your own channel's videos — [Trendo guide](https://www.trendo.studio/blog/link-shorts-to-long-videos/) (vendor, snippet only)
- A link/buy ask correlates with fewer views (0.82x) in the Content Labs dataset; the study describes this as costing reach — [The Content Labs, 17 Nov 2026](https://thecontentlabs.app/blog/call-to-action-data-study) (vendor)
- Since October 2026 YouTube's Shorts recommendations "further prioritize original content" and cut reach for re-uploads of others' content — [Social Media Today, 1 Oct 2026](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-updates-shorts-algorithm-to-put-more-focus-on-original-content/831976/)
- Instagram reported that 75% of recommendations in the US come from original posts — [Instagram Creators blog, 30 Apr 2026](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/rewarding-original-creators-on-instagram) (snippet only for this figure)
- Instagram Trial Reels show a Reel to non-followers first — [Fliki](https://fliki.ai/blog/trial-reels-instagram) (vendor, snippet only)
- Vendor claim that short-form (TikTok, Reels, Shorts) drives about 35% of indie game wishlists has no visible methodology — [game-developers.org](https://game-developers.org/indie-game-steam-wishlists-sources) (vendor, snippet only)

### Inferences
- Plan the first series as a batch of roughly 20-30 Shorts before judging the concept; expect hundreds, not thousands, of views per Short early, with occasional outliers. One Short a week (the Inbound Shovel cadence) is evidence-backed for a narrated devlog; 2-4 a week is feasible if short bot-moment clips are mixed in.
- Because follower count matters little in swipe feeds and each video is tested on its own, packaging per video (first frame, premise line, title) is the main lever a new channel controls.
- To move viewers to GitHub/Discord: Shorts descriptions and comments are weak link surfaces and link asks cost reach, so the practical funnel is Short -> Related Video (a long-form "how it works / how to run it") -> repo and Discord links in that video's description and the channel page. A handful of long-form videos is therefore needed even in a Shorts-first plan.
- Original footage of your own bot is on the right side of both YouTube's and Instagram's 2026 originality changes.

### Gaps
- No official statement from YouTube (Creator Insider, Todd Beaupré, Rene Ritchie) on posting frequency, new-channel boosts, or Shorts-versus-long-form recommendation separation could be retrieved; two targeted searches returned only vendor content.
- No r/NewTubers or r/gamedev threads with measured time-to-traction were retrieved; time-to-traction remains anecdotal.
- No data on Shorts-to-GitHub or Shorts-to-Discord conversion rates was found.
- No official TikTok description of For You testing for new accounts was retrieved.

## 7. Monetization basics (context only)

### Takeaway
Monetization is distant and getting harder: YouTube currently needs 1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 watch hours or 10M Shorts views in 90 days, and for new applicants from 1 February 2027 that reportedly doubles to 8,000 hours or 20M Shorts views; TikTok's Creator Rewards needs 10,000 followers and only pays on videos of one minute or longer.

### Cited Findings
- YouTube Partner Program (official page, read Nov 2026): 1,000 subscribers and either 4,000 qualified public watch hours in the last 12 months or 10 million qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days; watch time from the Shorts feed does not count toward the 4,000 hours — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851?hl=en)
- Announced 10 August 2026: from 1 February 2027 new applicants need 8,000 watch hours or 20 million Shorts views (subscriber requirement stays at 1,000); existing members must accept new terms by 31 January 2027; to keep earning from the Shorts pool members must maintain 10 million Shorts views per 90 days — [Digital Music News, 10 Aug 2026](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2026/08/10/youtube-doubles-eligibility-requirements-partner-program/) (press; YouTube's own post not opened)
- The 500-subscriber tier (3 public uploads in 90 days plus 3,000 watch hours or 3 million Shorts views; unlocks fan funding and Shopping, not ad revenue) is reported unchanged — [Digital Music News](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2026/08/10/youtube-doubles-eligibility-requirements-partner-program/)
- YPP eligibility and Shorts revenue are computed on "engaged views", not the public view count introduced on 31 March 2025 — [TechCrunch, 26 Mar 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/26/youtube-is-changing-how-youtube-shorts-views-are-counted)
- Shorts over one minute with any copyright claim are blocked and cannot monetize — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877?hl=en)
- TikTok Creator Rewards Program: age 18+, at least 10,000 followers and 100,000 video views in the last 30 days; eligible videos must be original and at least one minute long; ads/sponsored content, Duet/Stitch, Photo Mode and watermarked videos are excluded; available only in a limited set of countries (lists conflict) — [Toptal guide](https://www.toptal.com/creator/post/how-to-join-the-tiktok-creator-rewards-program), [ttcalculator](https://ttcalculator.net/learn/creator-rewards-program/) (vendor, snippet only)
- One 2026 source puts Creator Rewards at about $0.40-$1.00 per 1,000 qualified views — [Soundstripe](https://www.soundstripe.com/blogs/how-much-does-tiktok-pay-per-view) (vendor, snippet only)
- Mojang permits ad revenue from Minecraft videos that are free to view and contain the creator's own unique content — [Minecraft Usage Guidelines](https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/usage-guidelines)

### Inferences
- For a channel starting in late November 2026, the 1 February 2027 thresholds are the ones that will apply in practice; Shorts-only monetization (20M engaged views in 90 days) is unrealistic for a niche devlog, so monetization should not shape the format.
- If TikTok rewards ever matter, cuts must be 60 s or longer and watermark-free, which matches the 60-100 s narrated devlog length suggested by other evidence.

### Gaps
- TikTok's official Creator Rewards page could not be opened; thresholds come from vendor guides that agree with each other.
- Whether the creator's country is eligible for TikTok Creator Rewards or Instagram payouts was not checked (and should not be assumed).
- Instagram/Facebook Reels payout programs were not researched.
